The nicest part of WallCab is that it does not need to become another app on your phone. Apple already ships the pieces we need: a way to fetch a URL, a way to set a wallpaper, and a way to run those actions on a schedule.

## The entire Shortcut

The finished Shortcut contains only two actions:

1. **Get Contents of URL** points at the address created by the WallCab configurator.
2. **Set Wallpaper Photo** uses the result as your Lock Screen image.

That is the whole mechanism. There is no embedded token, account identifier, script, or opaque payload.

## Make your address

On the WallCab homepage, choose one or more learning interests, a visual theme, and an iPhone size. The configurator turns those preferences into a readable URL:

```text
/api/wallpaper?categories=science,history,psychology&theme=space&size=max
```

Copy the full address. If you ever want to change the edition, replace the relevant word in the Shortcut or return to the configurator and copy a new one.

## Add the two actions

Open Shortcuts and create a new Shortcut called **WallCab Daily**. Search for **Get Contents of URL**, add it, and paste your WallCab address into the URL field. The default `GET` method is correct.

Then search for **Set Wallpaper Photo**. Select the Lock Screen you want WallCab to update. Open the action’s options and turn **Show Preview** off; otherwise iOS will ask you to approve the image every day.

Run the Shortcut once manually. This confirms that your selected Lock Screen accepts automated changes and gives iOS the permissions it needs.

## Add the automation

Go to the Automation tab, make a personal automation, and choose **Time of Day**. Pick a time after WallCab’s UTC rollover, select **Daily**, and choose **Run Immediately**. Add your WallCab Daily Shortcut as the action.

If your preferred morning time falls before `00:05 UTC`, that is fine—the URL still returns the most recently prepared stable edition. The important part is consistency, not a particular clock time.

## Why this stays deliberately plain

A plain, inspectable URL is a durable interface. It works in Shortcuts, a browser, `curl`, and any future automation tool that can download an image. It can be copied, audited, or replaced without asking WallCab to remember who you are.

That is the larger design idea: useful automation should feel like a small tool you own, not a relationship you have to maintain.
