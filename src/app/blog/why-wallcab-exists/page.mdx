Most learning products begin by asking for a commitment. Make an account. Choose a goal. Turn on reminders. Protect a streak. Open a feed. These can be useful structures, but they also turn curiosity into another system to maintain.

WallCab began with a smaller question: what if one useful idea could simply be present?

## The glance already exists

We look at the lock screen throughout the day. That behavior does not need another notification or a new habit loop. A quiet piece of information can occupy the glance that already happens, then disappear when the day changes.

Repetition does the rest. A term seen in the morning feels familiar by lunch. A quotation becomes easier to test against the day. A scientific concept lingers without demanding that you finish a lesson.

## Small enough to finish

Each edition has a concept, a concise explanation, and one quotation, with room for an optional personal note. That boundary is important. WallCab is not trying to replace a book, course, or thoughtful article. It is trying to make the next book or article easier to enter.

The product itself follows the same principle. There are no accounts in the MVP, no billing system, no social layer, and no archive that needs to be managed. The browser remembers the wallpaper preferences. Apple Shortcuts does the automation.

## A cabinet, not a feed

The name comes from the idea of a wall cabinet: a fixed frame with changing, carefully chosen contents. A cabinet has edges. It asks someone to decide what belongs inside. It can be revisited without being infinite.

That metaphor guides both the visual identity and the technical architecture. Content comes from public sources, but it is validated before display. Photography carries its license. Fallback lessons are reviewed. The system is open source so its boundaries can be inspected.

WallCab is a modest tool. That is the point. It wants to leave you with an idea, then get out of the way.
